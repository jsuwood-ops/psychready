Curriculum overview

PsychReady is built around observable competencies, not one-off lectures.

1. Competency-based design

Map role-specific competencies for each staff group — assessment, communication, de-escalation, risk, medication safety, trauma-informed care, and equity. Link each to a personal development plan and refresh at induction, annually, and after incidents.

2. Behavioral Skills Training (BST)

Instruction → modeling → rehearsal → feedback, repeated until staff meet a defined mastery standard on an observed checklist. Your facility sets the threshold.

3. Simulation with structured debrief

Standardized-patient or role-play scenarios covering aggression, suicide risk, capacity, and ED crises, followed by a structured debrief. Interprofessional scenarios bring nursing, psychiatry, social work, and security together. One published quality-improvement project in a psychiatric emergency setting reported fewer restraints and staff injuries after simulation-based training combined with crisis-prevention content. That is one site's result, and other facilities may not see the same.

4. mhGAP-style cascade (“train the trainer”)

The WHO mhGAP Intervention Guide and training manuals use cascade training: master trainers prepare local facilitators, who train frontline staff through role-play, case discussion, and skills assessment. This helps training outlast staff turnover.

5. Clinical supervision as the backbone

Regular, competency-based supervision with direct observation, documented feedback, and post-incident reflection that supports staff wellbeing.

6. Aligned with APA, SAMHSA, and NIMH

Each module maps to public guidance: APA practice guidelines for clinical care, SAMHSA for crisis care, trauma-informed care and substance use, and NIMH for suicide screening and medication basics. Modules list their sources.

Standards & sources

PsychReady modules are aligned with public guidance from three U.S. organizations. We summarize; we don't replace their documents or your local policy.

  • American Psychiatric Association (APA): APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, APA Telepsychiatry resources
  • Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA): SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance, SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches, SAMHSA 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, SAMHSA Substance Use Treatment Options
  • National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH): NIMH Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) Toolkit, NIMH Suicide Prevention, NIMH Mental Health Medications

PsychReady is independent and not endorsed by APA, SAMHSA, or NIMH.
