Physical health deterioration in psychiatric settings

Module 7 · Audience: nurses, techs, and prescribing clinicians · Suggested time: 75-minute lab plus a 20-minute e-module

Learning objectives

  • Check airway, breathing, circulation, and consciousness when someone looks unwell.
  • Avoid explaining new physical signs only by the psychiatric diagnosis.
  • Use the facility early-warning score and escalation policy.
  • Name common medication adverse effects using a plain-language source and the facility medication list.
  • Escalate co-occurring substance-use emergencies without delaying medical care.

Learn

Diagnostic overshadowing means a physical problem is missed because staff attribute it to a psychiatric diagnosis or to behavior. New confusion, chest pain, trouble breathing, a stiff or very hot body, collapse, or a sudden change in alertness needs the same medical response you would use anywhere else.

Use your facility's early-warning score and the escalation steps that go with it. Do not invent a threshold for this lesson. NIMH Mental Health Medications is a plain-language overview of mental health medications and adverse effects, not a prescribing guide. For co-occurring substance use, SAMHSA Substance Use Treatment Options describes treatment options. Withdrawal and overdose are medical emergencies first.

This lesson is for recognition and escalation. It is not a protocol for doses or for choosing a medicine.

Standards: Medication adverse-effect content draws on NIMH's plain-language medication guide and APA practice guidelines; co-occurring substance use links to SAMHSA treatment resources. Sources: NIMH Mental Health Medications, APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, SAMHSA Substance Use Treatment Options.

Practice

Behavioral Skills Training:

  1. Instruct a head-to-toe concern list: airway, breathing, circulation, consciousness, temperature, and glucose if that is in your policy.
  2. Model an escalation call that leads with the physical signs, not the diagnosis.
  3. Rehearse reading your own early-warning tool and stating the action it requires.
  4. Feedback on whether the psychiatric diagnosis delayed the medical call.

Simulate

Simulation: a person described as just anxious who is short of breath and sweaty, and a second scene in which new confusion is blamed on a medication the team has not checked. Debrief overshadowing, who was called, and what was documented.

Standards & sources

PsychReady modules are aligned with public guidance from three U.S. organizations. We summarize; we don't replace their documents or your local policy.

  • American Psychiatric Association (APA): APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, APA Telepsychiatry resources
  • Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA): SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance, SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches, SAMHSA 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, SAMHSA Substance Use Treatment Options
  • National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH): NIMH Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) Toolkit, NIMH Suicide Prevention, NIMH Mental Health Medications

PsychReady is independent and not endorsed by APA, SAMHSA, or NIMH.
