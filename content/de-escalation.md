# De-escalation & crisis prevention

Module 1 · Audience: all direct-care staff, security · Suggested time: 90-minute skills lab plus a 20-minute e-module

## Learning objectives

- Recognize early signs of escalating distress and agitation.
- Use verbal and non-verbal de-escalation techniques.
- Coordinate team roles during an escalating situation.
- Explain when and how restrictive interventions may be used, only as a last resort under local policy.
- Take part in post-incident debrief and support.

## Why it matters

Agitation and aggression are common in emergency and inpatient psychiatric settings. They put patients and staff at risk, and restrictive interventions can cause harm and damage trust. Consensus guidance, such as the American Association for Emergency Psychiatry's Project BETA de-escalation workgroup, recommends verbal de-escalation as a first-line approach.

## Core skills

- **Respect personal space**: keep a safe distance and an open exit for everyone.
- **Avoid provocation**: calm posture, hands visible, matched eye level.
- **One communicator**: a single designated staff member leads.
- **Concise, simple language**: short sentences, repeat as needed.
- **Identify wants and feelings**: ask, listen actively, reflect back.
- **Agree or agree to disagree**: find truth to align with.
- **Set clear limits**: respectfully, with consequences explained.
- **Offer choices and optimism**, including food, drink, quiet space, or medication by choice.
- **Debrief** the patient and the staff afterward.

## Practice drills (BST format)

- **Instruct** (10 min): review the core skills.
- **Model** (10 min): facilitators demonstrate the "waiting room escalation" scenario.
- **Rehearse** (40 min): rotating triads of patient, communicator, and observer across three scenarios (ED boarding frustration, family visitor conflict, intoxicated patient).
- **Feedback** (20 min): the observer scores the checklist and the group debriefs (what went well, what to change, safety concerns).
- **Team drill** (10 min): call for help, assign roles, and run a closed-loop communication check.
