Behavioral health staff training checklist

A printable checklist for staff training in inpatient and residential behavioral health facilities. Use it for onboarding, annual competencies, and direct observation of high-risk skills.

Staff name _______________   Role _______________   Unit _______________
Date _______________   Preceptor _______________

Check a box only after a preceptor has seen the skill. Reading a module is not sign-off. Your facility sets the mastery threshold, for example all safety items plus most others. Reassess at one month. Refresh at induction, annually, and after incidents.

The onboarding and annual tables are the program record: one mark when observation of that competency is done. The high-risk tables are the skills the preceptor watches. Skip any row the role does not perform. Marks stay in this browser tab and are not sent to PsychReady. Print a copy if you need a record.

This checklist is a training aid. It is not a regulation, and it does not replace local policy, accredited training, or the public sources linked below.

Onboarding

In the first weeks, cover de-escalation, suicide and self-harm risk, trauma-informed care, and physical-health escalation. Pair each new hire with a preceptor. When the role includes crisis teamwork, video visits, or a situation where restriction might be considered, observe those high-risk tables during orientation as well.

Annual competencies

Hold an annual skills lab and re-observe the competencies this role uses. Between labs, a 10-minute huddle can rehearse one skill. After an incident, run a targeted refresher rather than waiting for the next annual date.

High-risk skills

Use these tables while you watch. Check a box only for a skill you saw. For de-escalation, suicide response, and medical escalation, withhold sign-off when a safety item is not met.

De-escalation and crisis prevention

Physical intervention stays a last resort under local policy.

Standards: Aligns with SAMHSA crisis care guidance (least-restrictive, recovery-oriented response) and APA practice guidelines on evaluation and care. Sources: SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance, APA Clinical Practice Guidelines.

Suicide risk screening and safety planning

Use a structured screen, such as NIMH's ASQ toolkit, and explain that a screen is not a full assessment. If someone is in immediate danger, follow your emergency policy rather than handing them a number and leaving.

Standards: Uses NIMH's free ASQ screening toolkit for medical settings, NIMH suicide-prevention guidance, APA's Psychiatric Evaluation of Adults guideline, and SAMHSA's 988 Lifeline for follow-up. Sources: NIMH Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) Toolkit, NIMH Suicide Prevention, APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, SAMHSA 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

Trauma-informed care

Explain what will happen before it happens, and offer a real choice. Use a qualified interpreter rather than a family member when clinical information is shared.

Standards: Built on SAMHSA's trauma-informed approach: safety; trustworthiness and transparency; peer support; collaboration; empowerment, voice and choice; and cultural, historical and gender issues. Sources: SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches.

Restraint and seclusion safety

These items are prevention and least-restrictive practice. This checklist does not authorize restraint or seclusion. Your facility policy, and the law where you practice, decide if and when those measures may be used.

Standards: Mirrors SAMHSA's trauma-informed and crisis care guidance, which favors prevention and least-restrictive options. Sources: SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches, SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance.

Telepsychiatry

Use this table only when the role includes video visits. Stop the visit when you cannot examine what matters, when agitation cannot be managed through the screen, when delirium or another medical emergency is possible, or when the technology fails.

Supervision

Sign-off means a supervisor saw the skill and the safety items were met. Attendance is not sign-off.

Standards: Supervisors check practice against APA practice guidelines and SAMHSA trauma-informed principles. Sources: APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches.

Teamwork

These items borrow brief, huddle, debrief, SBAR, call-out, and closed-loop tools from TeamSTEPPS, the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality teamwork program. They are not a substitute for an AHRQ course. Use them on inpatient units, in residential programs, and in the psychiatric emergency department.

Standards: Teamwork drills support the coordinated, no-wrong-door crisis response described in SAMHSA crisis care guidance. Sources: SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance.

Medical escalation

The supervision module names medical escalation, with de-escalation and suicide response, as a high-risk skill. This table is recognition and escalation. It is not a protocol for doses or for choosing a medicine. Use the facility early-warning score and the steps that go with it.

Standards: Medication adverse-effect content draws on NIMH's plain-language medication guide and APA practice guidelines; co-occurring substance use links to SAMHSA treatment resources. Sources: NIMH Mental Health Medications, APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, SAMHSA Substance Use Treatment Options.

Standards & sources

PsychReady modules are aligned with public guidance from three U.S. organizations. We summarize; we don't replace their documents or your local policy.

  • American Psychiatric Association (APA): APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, APA Telepsychiatry resources
  • Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA): SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance, SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches, SAMHSA 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, SAMHSA Substance Use Treatment Options
  • National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH): NIMH Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) Toolkit, NIMH Suicide Prevention, NIMH Mental Health Medications

PsychReady is independent and not endorsed by APA, SAMHSA, or NIMH.
