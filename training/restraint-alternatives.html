Restraint alternatives & human-rights aligned care

Module 6 · Audience: direct-care staff and security · Suggested time: 75-minute lab plus a 15-minute e-module

Learning objectives

  • Try prevention and environmental changes before any restrictive intervention.
  • Offer sensory, space, and choice-based options.
  • Explain a limit and why it exists.
  • Use restrictive measures only as a last resort under local policy.
  • Hold a post-incident review that includes the person when they can take part.

Learn

SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches and SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance favor prevention and the least restrictive response. Restrictive interventions can injure people and damage trust. This lesson does not authorize restraint or seclusion. Your facility policy, and the law where you practice, decide if and when those measures may be used.

Prevention includes noise, crowding, hunger, pain, fear, and unclear rules. Sensory and environmental options include a quieter space, a drink, a blanket, dimmer light, and a route that is not a dead end. After any incident, review what was tried, who was hurt or frightened, and what the team will change. Invite the person into that review when they are able to join.

Standards: Mirrors SAMHSA's trauma-informed and crisis care guidance, which favors prevention and least-restrictive options. Sources: SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches, SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance.

Practice

Behavioral Skills Training:

  1. Instruct a prevention checklist for the room and the first four non-restrictive options your unit actually has.
  2. Model offering two options before stating a limit.
  3. Rehearse an environmental scan: exits, noise, audience, and weapons or hazards.
  4. Feedback on whether restriction was discussed before other options were tried.

Simulate

Simulation: a person pacing and shouting in a crowded alcove. The team reduces the audience, offers space and a drink, assigns one communicator, and only then states a limit from policy. Debrief the order of those steps and the post-incident review.

Standards & sources

PsychReady modules are aligned with public guidance from three U.S. organizations. We summarize; we don't replace their documents or your local policy.

  • American Psychiatric Association (APA): APA Clinical Practice Guidelines, APA Telepsychiatry resources
  • Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA): SAMHSA National Behavioral Health Crisis Care Guidance, SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Approaches, SAMHSA 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, SAMHSA Substance Use Treatment Options
